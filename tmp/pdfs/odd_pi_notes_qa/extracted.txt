Abstract
We develop a nested-interval version of a classical logarithmic Hermite–Padé construc￾tion and give explicit upper bounds for the irrationality exponents of odd powers of π.
The construction uses Gaussian-integer polynomials in log i = iπ/2, rather than a direct
sequence of binary linear forms in 1, πk
. We prove integrality, an exact nonvanishing de￾terminant identity, a prime-divisor estimate described by weighted arcs on a circle, and
uniform analytic bounds. A minimal-polynomial resultant argument handles every ratio￾nal approximation, including reducible-binomial and rational-power cases. A twelve-layer
choice gives

The numerical inequalities are certified by ball arithmetic. Detailed background, worked
examples, the order of parameter choices, and unsuccessful finite experiments are included

1.5 A guide to the evidence
The algebraic and analytic lemmas prove Theorem 1.1. Ball-arithmetic certificates prove
the decimal comparisons once the lemmas hold. Exact finite examples check identities and
implementation, but do not prove asymptotic rates. Floating-point searches select parameters;
they neither certify rounding nor prove global optimality. The appendices retain unsuccessful
experiments because they explain the arithmetic difficulties that remain.

7 How the constants are computed and certified
7.1 An exact finite combinatorial calculation
The common divisor does not require numerical integration over a circle. The calculation is
finite:
1. Form the integer slopes 0, tj , L − tj and all nonzero differences.
2. Form and sort the rational critical points a/d ∈ [0, 1].
3. In each open critical cell choose its rational midpoint z. Sort all fractional endpoints
{tjz}, {(L − tj )z}.
4. Evaluate the integer vector W(z, y) at each rational midpoint between consecutive end￾points. Minimize its weighted sum.
5. Apply the finite digamma sum (3.5). Adjacent cells with the same minimum may be
merged.

B.4 Corrections that should not become unproved claims
A pointwise local-product limit is not a global steepest-descent proof. For example, replacing
the balanced G0 by
G0 + M log cos π
4M
at m = 289, M = 290 changes it by only −0.001063536257139 . . .. Even if justified, direct
substitution gives 2028.126464240039 . . ., not roughly 2018. No such sharpened oscillatory
estimate is used here.
Finite gcd samples also do not prove that an exponential divisor is present or absent.
Our main prime-divisor lower bound is proved coefficientwise and does not depend on a gcd
regression.
